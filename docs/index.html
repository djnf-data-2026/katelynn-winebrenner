2024 Frederick County School Board Election

In the 2024 Frederick County School Board election, the Frederick County Democratic Party endorsed four candidates for three open at-large seats. This decision caused splintering within the party that led to conservative candidates – Jamie Kiersten Brennan and Colt Morningstar Black – to take two of the seats.

Brennan stepped down as chair of the county chapter of Moms for Liberty to run for school board seat. She won 16.8% of the total votes– the highest in the county.

Janie Monier was the only candidate with support from the Democratic party to win, racking up 16.7% – the second highest percentage of votes.

Black ran with conservative support. He won 16.2% of the votes – the third top candidate.

Josh Bokee lost the race with 16.1% of the total votes. Chad King Wilson Sr. also lost with 14.8%. Veronica Lowe won the lowest 12.5%.

In rural precincts that voted overwhelmingly for President Donald Trump’s second term, Colt and Brennan performed well.

In precincts within the City of Frederick, where former Vice President Kamala Harris won an overwhelming majority, candidate Josh Bokee and Veronica Lowe performed well. However, neither candidate earned enough votes to secure standing in the top three.

In precincts where Harris narrowly outperformed Trump, the vote for the Democratic-backed candidates split, allowing Brennan and Black to place in the top three.

Monier was the top Democrat-supported candidate in these precincts, earning her a seat on the board.

Potential impact

The potential impact of this story would be to reveal key areas where splintering occurred to inform candidates and voters where the discrepancies were to increase voter participation and civic engagement in these areas.

Interest

This story was particularly surprising to me because it showcases the impact of partisanship within the county school board, which is technically considered nonpartisan. The extreme ideological differences between the candidates is also intriguing to me, particularly because the former Frederick County Moms of Liberty stepped down to run for a seat and won. I’m also interested in this story because it shows that, despite Frederick’s growth in recent years that have skewed its politics to the left, the conservative values in the county still remain strong.

Businesses to watch

Frederick is teeming with biotech and medical research companies. The Frederick National Laboratory for Cancer Research is one of the biggest non-governmental employers with 2,440 workers.

The Lab is operated by Leidos Biomedical Research, Inc. for the National Cancer Institute. According to its website, Leidos has operated there for over 25 years

Based on EDGAR search results for Leidos Holdings, Inc., which is an American Fortune 500 science, engineering, and information technology company, I found the company’s lastest 10-K annual filings from this February. Leidos Inc. itself did not have any 10-K filings in the last five years.

The company, which often serves as a contractor for the U.S. government, listed federal budget changes as a significant risk.

“A decline in the U.S. government budget, changes in spending or budgetary priorities or delays in contract awards may significantly and adversely affect our future revenues and limit our growth prospects.”

Specifically in terms of its health technologies I found:

“Health & Civil represented 30% for both fiscal 2025 and 2024 and 27% of total revenues for fiscal 2023.”

According to its 8-K from April this year, the company’s first quarter profit was 335 million dollars. Down from last year’s 363 million dollars, despite earning more revenue, 4.4 billion dollars this year compared to last year’s 4.245 billion dollars.

Health in particular brought in 1.19 billion dollars of revenue this first quarter, the same as last year’s quarter. “Health profitability was also relatively stable across periods.”
