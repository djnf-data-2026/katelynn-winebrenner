2024 Frederick County School Board Election

In the 2024 Frederick County School Board election, the Frederick County Democratic Party endorsed four candidates for three open seats. This decision caused splintering within the party that led to conservative candidates backed by Moms for Liberty to take two of the seats.

Relevance

This story is relevant now because no one has mapped out these votes yet to identify where the splintering happened, and there is another upcoming school board election for the other four seats.

general <- rbind(general1, general2)
 general_cleaned <- general |>
  select(cast_vote_record, precinct, ballot_style, seat_one = board_of_education_500001671, seat_two = x13, seat_three = x14)

seat_one <- general_cleaned |> select(cast_vote_record, precinct, ballot_style, candidate = seat_one)
seat_two <- general_cleaned |> select(cast_vote_record, precinct, ballot_style, candidate = seat_two)
seat_three <- general_cleaned |> select(cast_vote_record, precinct, ballot_style, candidate = seat_three)

combined <- union(seat_one, seat_two,)
combined <-union(combined, seat_three)
precinct_vote_counts <- combined |>
   group_by(precinct, candidate)|>
   arrange(candidate)|>
  summarize(votes=n()) |>
  mutate(precinct= substring(precinct, 2))|>
  filter(
    !is.na(candidate),
    !candidate %in% c("undervote", "overvote", "or write-in:") #questionable
  ) |>
  group_by(precinct) |>
  arrange(desc(votes), .by_group = TRUE) |>
  mutate(rank = row_number()) |>
  ungroup()


precinct_vote_wide <- precinct_vote_counts |>
  pivot_wider(
    names_from = rank,
    values_from = c(candidate, votes),
  names_glue = "{.value}_{rank}")|>
 mutate(

Potential impact

The potential impact of this story would be to reveal key areas where splintering occurred to inform candidates and voters where the discrepancies were to increase voter participation and civic engagement in these areas.

Interest

This story was particularly surprising to me because it showcases the impact of partisanship within the county school board, which is technically considered nonpartisan. The extreme ideological differences between the candidates is also intriguing to me, particularly because the former Frederick County Moms of Liberty stepped down to run for a seat and won. I’m also interested in this story because it shows that, despite Frederick’s growth in recent years that have skewed its politics to the left, the conservative values in the county still remain strong.

Previous coverage

From what I’ve seen, there has not been much coverage on this race in particular, other than generalized statewide coverage about the Democrats’ failures in school boards throughout the state from Maryland Matters.
